# Gromofon
### An ESP32-controlled plasma speaker

Gromofon is a project exploring how an electric arc can become a musical instrument. Instead of moving a conventional speaker cone, it produces sound by modulating the heating of the air around the arc.

The project is currently being designed. No working HV prototype has been tested yet.

## Why Gromofon?

The name combines two Polish words:
- **Gramofon** — a record player.
- **Grom** — thunder or a thunderbolt.

Together, they connect music with electrical discharges.

## Why this project?

I’m interested in high-voltage electronics and wanted to use that interest to build something that plays music. My first goal is to produce a clear test tone and then play a short melody through an electric arc.

## How it would work

1. **Audio source**  
   The first tests will use tones and melodies generated by the ESP32. An external audio input is planned, with Bluetooth as a possible later addition.

2. **ESP32 control**  
   The ESP32 generates a high-frequency switching signal and varies its duty cycle to modulate the power delivered to the arc. The switching frequency and maximum pulse duration must be matched to the transformer.

3. **Gate driver and MOSFET**  
   A TC4420 gate driver converts the ESP32’s logic signal into a suitable gate-drive signal for the power MOSFET. The current design shares a common ground between the ESP32 and the power stage

4. **High-voltage transformer**  
   The MOSFET switches the primary winding of a flyback transformer, which produces the voltage needed to sustain an arc between two electrodes.

5. **Sound generation**  
   Changes in heating make the surrounding air expand and contract, producing sound.

## Current circuit design

<img width="1317" height="871" alt="schematic" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d7e94368-4780-43d0-abf4-5d1495f97c87" />


The schematic currently includes:

- 24 V DC input.
- Input filtering capacitors.
- An L7812CV regulator supplying 12 V to the gate driver.
- A TC4420 gate driver.
- An IRFP260N as the proposed power MOSFET.
- A gate resistor and an input pull-down resistor.
- An RCD clamp intended to limit drain-voltage spikes.

Further schematic corrections include a gate-to-source pull-down resistor and local decoupling around the voltage regulator.

The transformer has not been selected. The RCD clamp values, operating frequency, maximum pulse duration and power level are therefore not finalized.

The prototype is planned for perfboard assembly, with short gate-drive connections and the high-voltage wiring kept separate from the control electronics.

## Planned features

- Test tones and stored melodies.
- External audio input through a 3.5 mm jack.
- Firmware limits on switching parameters.
- Start/stop control, with the output disabled at startup.
- Optional Bluetooth audio, depending on the selected ESP32 variant and implementation.
- A 3D-printed enclosure and mounting parts.

## Safety and limitations

This project involves high voltage, hot electrodes, UV radiation and gases produced by the arc. Guarding, ventilation, appropriate insulation and safe power disconnection must be considered.

The current design does not include hardware cycle-by-cycle current limiting. Firmware limits and an input fuse do not provide equivalent protection, so component damage remains a risk.

The circuit and its protection components have not yet been validated. This repository documents an experimental work in progress, not a verified build guide.
